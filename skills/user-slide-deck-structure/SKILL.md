# Slide Deck Structure

## Purpose and scope

Define audience, decision or learning goal, duration, format, source evidence, presenter context, and whether the deck is informational or persuasive.

## Workflow

Create a one-sentence throughline; sequence context, tension, evidence, insight, options, recommendation, and next step; assign one purpose per slide; choose the least misleading visual; write concise on-slide text and speaker notes; mark citations and asset rights; plan Q&A and accessibility.

## Verification and quality checks

Check narrative flow, claim-to-source traceability, slide density, legibility, contrast, color meaning, timing, speaker-note alignment, and whether the conclusion follows the evidence.

## Common errors

Common errors include slide-by-slide dumping, unsupported claims, decorative charts, unreadable citations, too many messages per slide, and confusing a template’s visual style with a coherent story.

## Rules, safety, and non-goals

Use original, public-domain, or licensed assets and respect attribution. Do not copy proprietary templates or manipulate evidence deceptively. Do not invent sources, data, results, approvals, or completed actions. Preserve privacy and use the smallest relevant skill composition.

## Handoff

Return audience and objective, slide outline, evidence and asset register, speaker notes, accessibility checks, timing, and review questions.
